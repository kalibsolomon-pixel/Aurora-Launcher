# Borealis pilot asset

`borealis.webp` is an original AI-generated aurora image produced with OpenAI ImageGen for this Phase F request on 2026-09-28. It contains no Lunar/Dawn artwork and no downloaded third-party footage. The owner-supplied Aurora logo was not used as an input and remains unchanged.

Direction: photorealistic northern lights, fine mint/teal/violet curtains above dark distant mountains; quiet dark center/lower area; no text, logo, watermark or UI. Generated master: 1672×941. Bundled WebP: 92,030 bytes. The generated master remains in the local Codex image output; only the optimized asset is needed at runtime.

The still remains the reduced-motion and decode-failure fallback. The original camera drift and subsequent distortion prototype have been removed.

`borealis-loop.mp4` is a locally rendered loop starting from this original artwork. It is **procedural internal-curtain animation, not recorded footage or output from an image-to-video model**. The 2026-09-30 corrective candidate replaces brightness-only variation with localized bending, stretching and contraction of fine curtain detail, plus travelling light that redistributes illumination between folds. Only the signed fine detail around a stationary broad emission envelope is remapped; the source image itself is never translated or warped. The largest detail sampling offsets are 17 horizontal and 12 vertical pixels at 1920×1080, vary locally, and taper to zero outside a fixed aurora support. Broad luminosity changes are removed from the animated delta so the entire curtain does not simply brighten and darken. The overall placement and macro silhouette remain anchored, with the landscape, sky and sharp star detail fixed. The published 1.3.0 asset is unchanged. This candidate requires owner review in the installed launcher before release work.

The silent H.264/yuv420p video is 1920×1080 at 30 fps for 20 seconds (600 frames), upscaled from the original 1672×941 source. Integer temporal harmonics (two and three cycles per loop) make both deformation and illumination periodic in value and velocity. Uncompressed frames at 0 and 20 seconds are identical; the encoded file omits the duplicate endpoint so playback does not hold an extra frame. The encoder consumes a lossless RGB master, encodes three periods with fixed closed GOPs and grain-preserving settings, and retains the middle period to avoid cold-start texture differences at the loop boundary. No crossfade, reverse playback, repositioning or reset is used. Keep the large authoring master and temporal evidence outside Git.

The current candidate is 6,372,600 bytes; SHA-256 `cb9a88c8a7dac6ca9c46ff5e296759f308013064c6969c75234e9e23d0145deb`. All 600 frames decode cleanly. The uncompressed last-to-first step averages 0.14638/255 per channel versus 0.14626/255 for the following step. At 960-pixel viewing width, the encoded seam averages 0.595/255, no larger than ordinary encoded keyframe transitions (0.597–0.632/255); it introduces no extra composition reset. Sampled broad luminous centroids vary by less than 2.4 source pixels, while stationary curtain regions change by 17–30/255 RMS over 2–6 seconds, including deformation that remains after fitting away uniform brightness changes. These measurements supplement temporal visual inspection; they do not replace owner acceptance.
Reproduce with `tools/visual/render-borealis.py --ffmpeg <executable> --output <mp4> --evidence <directory>`; add `--preview` for temporal stills and seam metrics without encoding. Pillow and NumPy are offline authoring tools only; the application adds no dependency or per-frame JavaScript work. A local video decoder owns playback, the stored motion setting maps to 0.5–1.5× playback, focus loss/hidden state pauses playback, reduced motion uses the still, and unmounting releases the source. Visual acceptance compares stationary curtain regions over 3–5 seconds at launcher scale, not merely the playback clock. No third-party preview footage is bundled.

The original image was generated for this project, not taken from an external asset library; there is no third-party attribution dependency. No claim of exclusive copyright in AI-generated imagery is made.
